the art of staying “locked in”

by teara · MAGDELINO · 2024

for gen z and many milennials, a new kind of hustle, fueled by desperation and a delusional kind of genius has emerged. for them, the “american dream” might as well be a best-selling masterpiece of turn-of-the-century classical creepypasta.

regardless of an individual’s given social status, what the economy has to offer young people in terms of opportunity to climb up to the next rung of the ladder we’ve clung to is typically either giving “cruel punchline” or “giant scam”.

“making it” isn’t even a tangible plan anymore— it’s a distant mirage that you scroll past on your phone. watching other people’s attempts at reenacting this picturesque fantasy would inspire puzzled bemusement, if it didn’t suck SO much.

mainstream culture stereotypes those who have opted out of the work force, or “neets”—those not in education, employment, or training—as couch-dwelling, vape-chiefing slackers. but let’s be real: a lot of these people are actually pushing themselves much harder and far further than any office drone. they’re just playing an entirely different game.

this isn’t their parent’s 9-to-5 anymore. it’s 24/7. instead of punching a clock, it’s about turning a hyperconnected, digital-first world into an adversarial playground out of collective boredom.

crypto’s US based anonymous participants, or “anons”, are spending 18 hour days flipping jpegs, using VPNs to day-trade major cryptocurrencies using absurdly high leverage, and are building their networks around personal brands by utilizing slanderous backroom gossip and feverishly mining the internet in pursuit of the next big thing to speculate on.

these hyper-niche skills aren’t exactly resumé-appropriate (yet), but they’re completely necessary if your goal is running up your score in these cartoonish, decentralized war games.

so, participants aren’t exactly slackers, but they may be something altogether worse. they’re dubiously motivated operators in a fast-moving, bloodthirsty economy driven by necessity, disillusionment, and the refusal to play by the old rules or to settle for scraps.

enter nfts. they’re not about "owning" a piece of digital art all—they, like meme coins, often emerge like diamonds in the rough, achieving outrageously high market capitalizations seemingly for no real reason. like their crate-digging ancestors, nft art fans and crypto degens easily scour a nauseating deluge of meaningless, crude, in-jokes and shit-posts, looking for a lottery ticket that could become a lifeline.

if you grew up sharing memes, are fluent in irony, and have spent far too much time spelunking the internet’s endless rabbit holes, it does kind of all start to make sense. value isn’t just about tangible assets or “companies" anymore; it’s now being extracted in its purest form. straight from the distillation of an idea, via culture, vibes, and uncanny connections we forge online.

it isn’t just about art, finance, or tech; in fact, it’s barely about any of those things. for all of its absurdity, the reality for irony poisoned young people is that there is meaning, opportunity, and a sliver of hope to be found in the absurd and irreverent, while the rest of the world follows bitterly with blinders on. the outcasts who have been living on the fringes of pop culture and the economy, outside of the status quo, are now somehow surviving in a world that often feels a lot like it’s bursting apart. still, navigating “the trenches” requires a thick skin, a slick sense of humor, and a near-pathological level of paranoia.

while chasing these insane risks might sound too stressful to be worth it, without other options it could sound preferable to living each day in stasis, constantly stuck between boredom and burnout. the familiar painful melancholy when confronted with the “normal” idea of working a meaningless, unstable career, for stagnant wages, for multiple decades, just to die (probably) alone and full of regret, is easy for anyone to become distracted by.
